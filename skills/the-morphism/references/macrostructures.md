# Macrostructures — page shapes

> The material (glass, neubrutalism, Swiss, or Bauhaus) dresses the shape. It does not choose it.
> Pick the page SHAPE first, then set the dials. This is the fix for "same page, different palette."

Two briefs should produce two different skeletons — not the same hero → features → CTA → footer run with a different blur or border width. A macrostructure is a complete page shape: heading placement, body composition, divider, button voice, image treatment, reveal. One named choice, not six decisions from scratch.

**Pick one. State it out loud before you write code:** *"Macrostructure: Bento Grid. Material: glass."*

**Refuse the last three.** No two consecutive builds in the same project share a macrostructure — and not two editorial variants of the same shape either. Pick from a categorically different family (grid-led vs document-led vs poster-led). If you built Bento Grid yesterday, today is Long Document or Manifesto, not "Bento Grid but with rounder corners."

The glass, neo, Swiss, and Bauhaus takes below are the material's read on the same shape. Glass keeps it cool and layered; neo keeps it loud and sharp; Swiss keeps it flat and ordered; Bauhaus keeps it flat and geometric. Same skeleton, different material.

## The 12 macrostructures

**01 · Bento Grid** — modular blocks of mixed spans (1×1, 2×1, 1×2, 2×2); rhythm comes from size, not card uniformity.
- Glass: frosted tiles over a rich image or gradient; each tile a translucent panel at the same blur.
- Neo: flat-color tiles with hard offset shadows; each tile owns one saturated accent, black border, square.
- Swiss: a strict grid, regular or near-regular spans, hairline rules between cells; hierarchy via opacity, not shadow.
- Bauhaus: a grid of flat primary-colored tiles and black rules; cells are geometric blocks, hierarchy via size and fill, not shadow.
- Reach for: "many things to show" — feature pages, SaaS, dashboards. Avoid: a single hero idea.

**02 · Marquee Hero** — one bold statement fills the fold. No subhead, no CTA above the fold. Below, the page becomes something else.
- Glass: the statement floats over a rich image or a single light-source gradient; text-shadow protection.
- Neo: giant loud display type, one saturated accent block, hard shadow under the statement.
- Swiss: one light-weight statement in huge type, vast whitespace, nothing else. The typographic poster.
- Bauhaus: one heavy uppercase statement in geometric type, a primary-color shape beside it, asymmetric. The poster.
- Reach for: launch pages, agency splashes, one-idea products. Avoid: content-heavy pages that need the fold to explain.

**03 · Split Diptych** — text on one side, proof on the other. The pairing alternates direction down the page.
- Glass: copy-left + frosted product panel right (the canonical glass hero — H7 clipped-edge variant).
- Neo: copy-left + hard-shadow card right; the divider is a 3px black rule, not whitespace.
- Swiss: text left, a data table or diagram right, on a hard 8/4 column split with a hairline divider.
- Bauhaus: text left, a geometric composition (circle/square/triangle) right, hard asymmetric split, black rule.
- Reach for: SaaS, dev tools, studio sites. Avoid: pure narrative with no visual proof.

**04 · Stat-Led** — a giant number is the hero; everything after supports or qualifies it.
- Glass: the number sits on a frosted slab over a dark backdrop; tabular numerals.
- Neo: the number in heavy display on a flat accent fill; a hard-shadow figure, black-on-yellow.
- Swiss: a huge light-weight tabular number with a small caption. The number is the poster.
- Bauhaus: a huge bold geometric number in a primary fill, a small caption. The number IS the poster.
- Reach for: fintech, metrics products, "the number is the pitch." Avoid: a brief with no real number — invented stats are slop.

**05 · Manifesto** — polemical large type, declaration energy. The page tells you what to believe before it tells you what to buy.
- Glass: rare — glass wants to be cool, not loud. Only if the brand is confident and cool by nature.
- Neo: NATIVE. This is the neo poster — heavy type, hard rules, one accent, "WE ARE NOT A PLATFORM."
- Swiss: a declarative typographic poster — large light type, one accent, no polemic, just the statement set in a grid.
- Bauhaus: NATIVE. A bold uppercase declaration in geometric type, primary-color blocks, hard diagonals. This is the Bauhaus poster.
- Reach for: studios, campaigns, causes, opinionated founders. Avoid: trust-first surfaces.

**06 · Long Document** — reads like a memo, letter, or journal entry. No marketing structure. Continuous prose with inline heads.
- Glass: a frosted reading panel over a quiet backdrop; the blur is a frame, not a feature.
- Neo: high-contrast black on off-white, hard rules between chapters, mono for the byline.
- Swiss: NATIVE. The canonical Swiss form — continuous prose, inline heads, hairline rules, a colophon at the foot.
- Bauhaus: continuous prose with heavy geometric inline heads and primary-color section rules; a colophon at the foot.
- Reach for: "this is literature about the product," personal sites, essays. Avoid: skim-and-scan SaaS.

**07 · Workbench** — product screenshots in frames are the primary content. A guided tour of the thing in use.
- Glass: screenshots in frosted frames; real captures, never redrawn chrome.
- Neo: screenshots in hard-shadow frames with a 1–3° tilt; chunky annotation pins.
- Swiss: screenshots in hairline frames with numbered caption pins. No chrome, no tilt — the Basel school's diagram-and-photograph.
- Bauhaus: screenshots in flat primary frames with numbered caption pins; the diagram-and-photograph in geometric frames.
- Reach for: dev tools, apps, "here's what you do with it." Avoid: no product to show.

**08 · Quote-Led** — the hero is a pull-quote with attribution. Borrowed credibility leads.
- Glass: the quote on glass over a dimmed image; attribution in a frosted chip.
- Neo: one huge quote, hard shadow, the attribution in small caps beneath.
- Swiss: a large light-weight quote, attribution in small caps beneath, a single hairline above.
- Bauhaus: a large heavy quote in a primary block, attribution in small caps beneath, a black rule above.
- Reach for: social-proof-first, testimonials-as-hero. Avoid: a brand that needs to lead with its own voice.

**09 · Photographic** — a single huge image dominates each fold. Text is small annotation, not headline.
- Glass: the canonical glass-over-image hero — the photo IS the backdrop the blur needs.
- Neo: rare — neo is shape and color, not photography. Only if the photo is the product itself.
- Swiss: full-bleed photography with a small caption in the margin — the Basel school's primary element.
- Bauhaus: full-bleed photography overlaid with primary-color geometric shapes; caption in the margin.
- Reach for: food, travel, fashion, anything visual-first. Avoid: typography-led brands.

**10 · Catalogue** — a uniform grid of variations of the same thing (SKUs, work, typefaces).
- Glass: uniform frosted cards; the glass flattens them into one calm surface.
- Neo: flat-color cards, one accent per item, hard shadows, square corners.
- Swiss: NATIVE. A uniform grid of specimens, hairline rules, tabular data, captions in small caps.
- Bauhaus: NATIVE. A uniform grid of specimens on primary tiles, black rules, captions in small caps.
- Reach for: portfolios, shops, collections. Avoid: a single narrative thread.

**11 · Letter** — first-person, written, intimate. Opens "Dear X,". No buttons in the fold.
- Glass: a frosted note card, quiet, personal. The blur is restraint.
- Neo: a bold letter, hard rules, signed in loud type. Intimacy at full volume.
- Swiss: a typeset letter — "Dear X," in light type, formal, one accent reserved for the signature.
- Bauhaus: a bold typeset letter, "Dear X," in heavy geometric type, a primary-color signature block.
- Reach for: personal sites, founder notes, hand-crafted brands. Avoid: transactional pages.

**12 · Portfolio Grid** — filterable cards of projects. The work is the product.
- Glass: frosted cards over a dark gallery; hover shifts opacity, never scales.
- Neo: hard-shadow cards, one accent per project, the "press" hover on each tile.
- Swiss: a strict grid of projects, hairline rules, caption + year in small caps.
- Bauhaus: a grid of projects on alternating primary tiles, black rules, caption + year in small caps.
- Reach for: studios, designers, agencies. Avoid: single-product marketing.

## Domain routing

When the brief is vague, offer three macrostructures from categorically different families — never default.

| Domain words | Offer these three | Material lean |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS · dev tool · infra · API · B2B | Bento · Workbench · Stat-Led | glass (premium) · neo (dev-tool playful) |
| agency · studio · portfolio · case studies | Portfolio Grid · Split Diptych · Catalogue | neo (loud) · glass (cool) |
| personal · about-me · resume | Long Document · Letter · Quote-Led | either |
| restaurant · food · café · atelier | Photographic · Letter · Catalogue | glass over photography |
| manifesto · campaign · cause · political | Manifesto · Stat-Led · Quote-Led | neo (native) |
| fintech · banking · trust-first | Stat-Led · Workbench · Long Document | glass (calm = trust) |
| editorial · docs · archive · museum · typography · reference | Long Document · Catalogue · Marquee Hero | Swiss (native) |
| arts · culture · music · education · architecture · poster | Catalogue · Manifesto · Marquee Hero | Bauhaus (native) |
| fallback (no signal) | Bento · Long Document · Manifesto | either |

If the brief has no domain word, you didn't read it carefully enough. Ask one question before you pick.
